Ethics and privacy.

A face is personal data. A feeling is not a measurement. These two sentences decide almost every design choice in this project.

No data stored Consent first Fairness tested
People first

Respect the person behind the pixels

Expression recognition touches something intimate. A careful system asks before it watches, keeps as little as it possibly can, and says plainly when it is unsure. Anything less turns a demonstration into surveillance.

In this concept, frames are processed in memory and discarded. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is saved to a disk, and no facial image leaves the device.

01

Consent

People should always know when the system is watching, and be able to say no without losing anything.

02

Collect less

If a feature can be computed from this frame and then forgotten, storing it is a choice — and the wrong one.

03

Be transparent

Every estimate is shown with its confidence, and every claim about accuracy is stated honestly.

04

Test fairly

Accuracy must be checked across skin tones, ages, disabilities and lighting — not just on the training set.

Hard limits

Four things this project refuses to do

Never claim a true feeling

The output is an estimate of visible pattern, not knowledge of an internal state. Every label on this site is worded that way on purpose.

Never score a person

No hidden ranking of who looks happy, engaged or difficult. Aggregates, where used at all, must never become profiles.

Never decide for someone

No grades, no access, no consequences. The robot may suggest; a human decides, and can always say no.

Never replace a person

If someone is actually struggling, the honest answer is a human being — not a robot's guess at a smile.

AI should assist, not assume. An expression estimate is one weak signal among many. It must never be the sole basis for a decision that affects somebody's life.
