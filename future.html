What could the robot learn next?

Each extra signal makes the estimate richer — and the ethical questions sharper. Every idea below comes with the safeguard it would need.

Voice Gesture Gaze
Next signals

Six directions, and their safeguards

Voice and tone

Pace, pauses and volume add real context that a face alone cannot show.

Safeguard: audio processed locally, never transcribed into a permanent record.

Gesture and posture

Folded arms or a raised hand can change the meaning of an expression entirely.

Safeguard: people must be able to see exactly what is being tracked.

Gaze and attention

Where somebody looks is useful for turn-taking in conversation.

Safeguard: gaze is the most sensitive signal of all — use it only with explicit consent.

Personal responses

Let each person choose how the robot speaks, and what it should ignore.

Safeguard: preferences stay on the device and are easy to erase.

More languages

Interaction in the language a person actually thinks in, not just the one the data was collected in.

Safeguard: test each language separately instead of assuming it transfers.

Better models

Higher accuracy, calibrated confidence, and the ability to say "I do not know".

Safeguard: publish the accuracy per group, not just as one overall number.

Roadmap

A realistic order of development

Now

Expression estimate only

One signal, made visible, with an honest confidence score. That is the version on this website.

Next

Add tone, with consent

Combine expression with voice energy so the estimate can correct itself when the two disagree.

Later

Context, then caution

Multi-signal understanding is the point at which a system starts feeling like it knows you — so that is where the strictest limits belong.

The goal is not a robot that knows how you feel. It is a robot that is careful, transparent and easy to correct — and that leaves the important judgements to people.
